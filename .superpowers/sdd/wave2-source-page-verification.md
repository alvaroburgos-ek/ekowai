## THE REVERSAL (R-5) — the brief's bulk-correction premise does not hold

The brief instructed: *"mechanical-offset blocks bulk-corrected where the pattern is proven."*

**Almost nothing qualifies. I applied no bulk correction, and that is the finding.**

Three independent tests, all re-runnable:

1. **The verifier already applies the per-document offset.** The `off` column *is* the derived
   mechanical offset (derived per PDF from footer/index evidence, never assumed; rejected below a
   confidence floor). Every OFF-BY-N figure is therefore the **residual after** mechanical
   correction. Bulk-shifting it again would be inventing precision, not recovering it.

2. **Residuals do not cluster per document.** Per-standard dominant-offset purity:
   only 8 standards reach purity 1.0 and they total **13 nodes** (pure only because n=1–4).
   The large populations scatter: `DWA-A-262E` 70 residuals across **14 distinct offsets**,
   `DWA-M-102-4` 48 across 7, `DWA-A-226` 41 across 5, `DWA-A-138-1` 38 across 5,
   `DWA-A-222` 34 across 10.

3. **The decisive test — the same claimed page yields different residuals.** A page-numbering
   offset is a property of the document and must be constant per page. It is not:
   - `DWA-A-222` printed p.20 → 9 nodes resolve at −1, 17 nodes at other offsets.
   - `DWA-A-262E` pages 18, 19, 25, 26, 31, 34, 44 each appear in **both** the dominant and the
     non-dominant set.
   - `DWA-A-226` pages 9 and 13, `DWA-M-102-4` pages 18 and 21, `DWA-A-138-1` page 42 — same.

   **Conclusion: this is not page drift. These are per-node citation defects** (and, in part,
   verifier token-noise — see residue). They must be adjudicated per node against the PDF.

**Per-node-class re-test** (blocks might be per class rather than per document — e.g. all `eq`
nodes generated by one bad inference). Corpus-wide, exactly **one** class-block clears a ≥0.9
purity / ≥5 node bar:

| standard | class | offset | ratio | purity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DVS-2225-4` | `dp` | +1 | 5/5 | 1.00 |

That is the entire legitimate bulk-correction surface in a 3 983-node corpus: **5 nodes.**
I have **not** applied it either — 5 nodes is below the threshold where a bulk write beats a
per-node check, and it belongs in the decision batch rather than in an unsupervised sweep.

## HONEST RESIDUE

1. **OFF-BY-N is a candidate defect, not a proven one.** The verifier locates a distinctive token
   on a *different* page than claimed. A token can legitimately recur, and a node may cite where a
   table *begins* while the token sits on the continuation page. **639 is an upper bound on real
   defects, not a count of them.** Wave-1's per-node audit of 138-1 confirmed both kinds (`tab-10`
   genuinely page-wrong; `eq-gl8` merely off-by-one). Nothing was downgraded on this signal alone.
2. **985 UNTESTABLE were not touched.** They are neither confirmed nor refuted. Calling them
   "verified" would be fabrication; calling them "wrong" would be equally unfounded.
3. **51.4 % is accuracy over the *testable* subset.** Over all 2 715 page-bearing nodes the
   confirmed fraction is **32.7 %**. Both numbers are true; the second is the one to quote when
   asking "what fraction of this corpus has a page citation proven today."
4. **No prod writes, no map downgrades applied this wave.** The wave produced measurement plus the
   six N-1 body-text repairs (below). Everything else is queued for signature.
5. **Waves 3+ (the improvement campaign) did not start.** Grounding + N-1 + a corpus-wide
   verification consumed the session. No standard was taken to READY-TO-USE. Stated plainly rather
   than implied by omission.
