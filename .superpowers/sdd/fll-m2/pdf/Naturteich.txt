For over three decades, various countries around the world (including Austria, Germany, Switzerland, Portugal, Russia and Australia)
have been building natural swimming pools of various types, using different construction methods, sometimes with patented systems.
These are artificially created ecosystems that are different to conventional swimming pools in that they use either full or partial biological
water filtration technology.
There are now more than 10,000 private natural swimming pools worldwide. According to the German Association for Natural Bathing
Waters (DGfnB), in recent times in Germany alone, about 600 private facilities every year have been built from scratch, or else expan-
ded or remodelled from existing pools built in the 1960s and 1970s. There is also a growing trend towards using biological water filtra-
tion in public swimming ponds.
Whereas conventional facilities are generally planned by architects and built by civil engineering and building construction firms, the
planning, construction and maintenance of natural swimming pools has become increasingly regarded as an area for landscape archi-
tects and gardening and landscaping companies.
The first “Recommendations for the Planning, Construction and Maintenance of Private Natural Swimming Pools” were published in
2006 by the Landscape Development and Landscaping Research Association (FLL), in its role as regulator of the Green Industry. They
have been well received in practice and are widely used.
Since that document was published, various research findings have been presented, many experiences have been gathered and
numerous swimming ponds have been built according to the recommendations.
Constant developments in the field of swimming pond construction have prompted the Landscape Development and Landscaping
Research Association (FLL) to collaborate with the German Association for Natural Bathing Waters (DGfnB) to revise the 2006 edition
and bring it into line with the latest technological developments.
As part of the process, the contents of the regulations were checked, adapted to the way the current legal framework is applied in prac-
tice and supplemented with newly acquired knowledge. They were then developed into the FLL’s revised “Guidelines for the Planning,
Construction and Maintenance of Private Natural Swimming Pools”.

                                                                                                                                                                          With the adoption of the Common Essentials in October 2021 by all incorporated national as-
                                                                                                                                                                          sociations of the IOB e. V. (International Organization for Natural Bathing Waters), the mem-
                                                                                                                                                                          bers for private bathing in water with biological treatment.

                                                                                                                                                                          For the first time, all member associations as well as associated associations have established
                                                                                                                                                                          and adopted 14 common international principles for planning, construction and maintenance.
                                                                                                                                                                          Existing codes of practice, such as these guidelines in particular, are fully reflected in the in-
                                                                                                                                                                          ternational Common Essentials. Thus, this translation of the guidelines for the design, con-
                                                                                                                                                                          struction and maintenance of private swimming and bathing ponds elaborates in detail the
                                                                                                                                                                          overarching guiding principles and provides a basis for all phases in the creation and operation
                                                                                                                                                                          process of swimming ponds and natural pools that has already been applied in various Euro-
                                                                                                                                                                          pean countries.

                                                                                                                                                                          For this reason, the IOB e. V., as the international umbrella organization for all forms of swim-
                                                                                                                                                                          ming facilities with targeted biological water treatment, welcomes and promotes the publication
                                                                                                                                                                          of this set of rules in English.

                                                                                                                                                                          This set of rules makes an important contribution, especially internationally, to the standardi-
                                                                                                                                                                          zation of both the terms and the recognized functional types of such water features.

                                                                                                                                                                          •   More than 30 years of practical experience and continuous development of private swim-
                                                                                                                                                                              ming ponds and natural pools have already been incorporated into this set of rules.
                                                                                                                                                                          •   With this set of rules, all professional groups involved in the planning or manufacturing
                                                                                                                                                                              process have access to the relevant framework conditions in a compact and well-founded
                                                                                                                                                                              manner.
                                                                                                                                                                          •   With regard to the protection and safety of users and third parties, the applicable country-
                                                                                                                                                                              specific laws, guidelines and directives for the place of manufacture must always be taken
                                                                                                                                                                              into account.

                                                                                                                                                                          In justified cases, it is advisable to adapt the framework conditions to the prevailing conditions
                                                                                                                                                                          at the place of installation. This may be the case in particular if the local climate deviates from
                                                                                                                                                                          the Central European cool-temperate climate zone. In addition, the regional autochthonous
                                                                                                                                                                          species and materials for the corresponding biocoenoses of the recognized functional types
                                                                                                                                                                          must always be taken into account.

                                                                                                                                                                          In addition to the members of the Rules Committee and Working Group, we would like to thank
                                                                                                                                                                          all those who contributed to the publication of this international edition.

                                                                                                                                                                          We hope that the translation now available in English will promote and further establish the
                                                                                                                                                                          trend of bathing in biologically treated water worldwide.

                                                                                                                                                                          Preface

                                                                                                                                                                          Natural swimming pools have being been built for more than three decades now in various
                                                                                                                                                                          countries of the world (e. g. in Austria, Germany, Switzerland, Portugal, Spain, Italy, Poland,
                                                                                                                                                                          Russia and Australia) in a variety of forms and designs, some of which feature patented sys-
                                                                                                                                                                          tems.

                                                                                                                                                                          They are artificially created eco systems in which the water is purified either fully or partially
                                                                                                                                                                          through biological processes – as opposed to conventional pools.

                                                                                                                                                                          Meanwhile, there are 10,000 private natural swimming pools worldwide. According to the in-
                                                                                                                                                                          formation provided by the Deutsche Gesellschaft für naturnahe Badegewässer e. V. (DGfnB -
                                                                                                                                                                          German association for semi-natural swimming waters) approx. 600 private pools have been
                                                                                                                                                                          installed every year in recent years in Germany alone either by the construction of new pools
                                                                                                                                                                          or through the upgrading or redesigning of existing pools built in the 1960s and 1970s. Fur-
                                                                                                                                                                          thermore, swimming pools with biological water purification in the area of public pools are also
                                                                                                                                                                          becoming more and more mainstream.

                                                                                                                                                                          Whereas conventional pools are normally designed by structural engineers and constructed
                                                                                                                                                                          by building construction firms or civil engineering companies, the planning, construction and
                                                                                                                                                                          maintenance of natural swimming pools has increasingly become established as a domain of
                                                                                                                                                                          landscape architects and gardening and landscaping companies.

                                                                                                                                                                          As in the case of all innovative fields of activity during the past 50 years – e. g. "green" roofs,
                                                                                                                                                                          interior landscaping, rainwater drainage and plant-based sewage systems – this field of activity
                                                                                                                                                                          is also associated with plenty of new challenges and continuous further development. The
                                                                                                                                                                          planning, construction and maintenance of natural swimming pools require comprehensive
                                                                                                                                                                          knowledge and experience, among other things regarding filtration, hydraulic systems and fluid
                                                                                                                                                                          mechanics, biology of waterbodies (in particular efficiency and stability of an eco system) as
                                                                                                                                                                          well as hygiene aspects.

                                                                                                                                                                          1          Area of application, purpose


                                                                                                                                                                          1.1        Area of application

                                                                                                                                                                          The “Richtlinien für Planung, Bau und Instandhaltung von privaten Schwimm- und Ba-
                                                                                                                                                                          deteichen" (guidelines for the planning, construction and maintenance of private natural swim-
                                                                                                                                                                          ming pools) apply to the planning and construction as well as operation, inspection, mainte-
                                                                                                                                                                          nance and repairs for open-air natural swimming pools – hereinafter referred to as natural
                                                                                                                                                                          pools:
                                                                                                                                                                          •     which are specifically built and operated for use as a natural swimming pool;
                                                                                                                                                                          •     which are exclusively used for private purposes;
                                                                                                                                                                          •     which have a seal between the water in the pool and subsoil;
                                                                                                                                                                          •     which are subject to requirements in terms of water quality;
                                                                                                                                                                          •     which feature systems for purifying the water biologically, possibly also physically and/or
                                                                                                                                                                                physically/chemically;
                                                                                                                                                                          •     which represent a closed system and do not have any permanent inflows or outflows.

                                                                                                                                                                          Installations which are used for commercial and public purposes and not exclusively for private
                                                                                                                                                                          purposes in accordance with § 37 of the Infektionsschutzgesetz (German Infection Protection
                                                                                                                                                                          Act) (e. g. hotel pools) are subject to the “Richtlinien für Planung, Bau, Instandhaltung und
                                                                                                                                                                          Betrieb von öffentlichen Schwimm- und Badeteichanlagen“ (guidelines for the planning, con-
                                                                                                                                                                          struction, maintenance and operation of public natural swimming pools).


                                                                                                                                                                          1.2        Purpose

                                                                                                                                                                          Natural pools differ from conventional swimming pools particularly in the fact that they are
                                                                                                                                                                          artificially created eco systems, in which the water is purified either in full or in part by biological
                                                                                                                                                                          processes. These processes can be supported by technical equipment. At the same time, one
                                                                                                                                                                          of the key goals is to ensure that the water meets minimum quality requirements. In terms of
                                                                                                                                                                          planning, construction and maintenance of natural pools, this requires that both knowledge of
                                                                                                                                                                          the relevant structural and technical installations are a pre-requisite, as are an understanding
                                                                                                                                                                          of the biological background – in particular the ecology of waterbodies – and the physical and
                                                                                                                                                                          chemical interactions and processes that take place during the purification of the water.

                                                                                                                                                                          The “Richtlinien für Planung, Bau, Instandhaltung und Betrieb von Freibädern mit biologischer
                                                                                                                                                                          Wasseraufbereitung” (guidelines for the planning, construction, maintenance and operation of
                                                                                                                                                                          open air natural swimming pools) which are already available only have limited use for the
                                                                                                                                                                          private sector, as the requirements required by law for public pools often entail requirements
                                                                                                                                                                          that are not compulsory in the private sector and can only – if at all – be taken as a guide for
                                                                                                                                                                          orientation purposes.

                                                                                                                                                                          The purpose of these guidelines is to define terminology and minimum standards for the plan-
                                                                                                                                                                          ning, construction and maintenance of private natural pools that safeguard the intended use
                                                                                                                                                                          of the pool, create contractual security and thus facilitate the cooperation between purchasers,
                                                                                                                                                                          planning engineers, suppliers and installation firms.

                                                                                                                                                                          Noncompliance or modifications can have significant disadvantages for planning engineers
                                                                                                                                                                          and construction companies – in particular of course also for the owner – e. g. a lack of legal
                                                                                                                                                                          clarity and differences of opinion relating to the agreed services, inadequate standards, shorter
                                                                                                                                                                          length of utilization and reduced resistance as well as increased follow-up costs for care and
                                                                                                                                                                          maintenance and/or reconstruction.

                                                                                                                                                                          3         Definitions

                                                                                                                                                                          Note:
                                                                                                                                                                          The definitions of some of the terms below may vary in practical use. However, it is hoped that
                                                                                                                                                                          these terms will become established, in order to facilitate the cooperation between purchasers,
                                                                                                                                                                          operators, planners, manufacturers and installation firms and to help form a more secure basis
                                                                                                                                                                          for contractual agreements. Other still currently used common terms are listed as “synonyms”.


                                                                                                                                                                          Regeneration area
                                                                                                                                                                          The area used for biological, physical and physical/chemical purification of the water which is
                                                                                                                                                                          not accessible to swimmers.
                                                                                                                                                                          It consists of the  plant zone and/or filtration zone as well as the water therein.
                                                                                                                                                                          Synonyms: Purification zone, regeneration zone


                                                                                                                                                                          Benthos
                                                                                                                                                                          Biocenosis in and on the bottom of a body of water.


                                                                                                                                                                          Feed rate
                                                                                                                                                                          Size of the hydraulic surface load with a controlled flow through the filter, usually specified as
                                                                                                                                                                          m3/(m2 h) or as m3/(m2 day).
                                                                                                                                                                          Synonyms: Hydraulic load, feed height


                                                                                                                                                                          Biofilm
                                                                                                                                                                          Accumulation of bacteria, other microorganisms and (under the influence of light) algae on
                                                                                                                                                                          damp or wet surfaces.


                                                                                                                                                                          Biocides/biocidal products
                                                                                                                                                                          Substances or mixtures that contain active components which cause a chemical or biological
                                                                                                                                                                          attack on organisms and not purely by means of physical or mechanical influence.


                                                                                                                                                                          Biocenosis
                                                                                                                                                                          Biocenosis of all different types in a definable habitat (biotope). Plants, animals, microorgan-
                                                                                                                                                                          isms in close relationship to one another. Together, biocenosis and biotope form the ecosys-
                                                                                                                                                                          tem.


                                                                                                                                                                          One Box system
                                                                                                                                                                          The areas of the pool intended for swimming and regeneration area are located within a single
                                                                                                                                                                          basin/pool.
                                                                                                                                                                          Note: Term that is no longer used in these guidelines.



                                                                                                                                                                          Supplementary area
                                                                                                                                                                          Areas which do not belong directly to the natural swimming pool but are added for their use,
                                                                                                                                                                          e. g. paths and access routes to the pool, rest areas and areas for lying down, playing areas
                                                                                                                                                                          for children.

                                                                                                                                                                          Filters
                                                                                                                                                                          Within these guidelines, a water treatment device used to separate materials as well as to
                                                                                                                                                                          break down organic substances and organisms through biological, physical and/or physical-
                                                                                                                                                                          chemical methods.

                                                                                                                                                                                Single-layer filter
                                                                                                                                                                                Substrate filter that consists of an effective filter layer (uniform grading curve and material
                                                                                                                                                                                composition).

                                                                                                                                                                                Multi-layer filter
                                                                                                                                                                                Substrate filter that consists of multiple effective grain sized filter layers or different
                                                                                                                                                                                filter materials.

                                                                                                                                                                                Substrate filter
                                                                                                                                                                                Technical installation for water purification, normally made of granular mineral materials
                                                                                                                                                                                and controlled flow through; can be with or without plants.
                                                                                                                                                                                Synonym: constructed wetlands


                                                                                                                                                                          Filter velocity
                                                                                                                                                                          Fictitious flow speed derived from Darcy’s law, when you form the quotient of flow rate Q to
                                                                                                                                                                          the total flow cross-section F of the porous medium. The filter velocity is not the actual water
                                                                                                                                                                          speed in the filter.
                                                                                                                                                                           Pore velocity


                                                                                                                                                                          Filter-effective layer
                                                                                                                                                                          In terms of these guidelines, the layer in a substrate filter in which the water is purified.


                                                                                                                                                                          Filter material
                                                                                                                                                                          Material (natural or synthetic) that has a controlled flow through and serves water purification.


                                                                                                                                                                          Flow velocity
                                                                                                                                                                          Synonym: Pore velocity


                                                                                                                                                                          Limnoecology concept
                                                                                                                                                                          Interaction of flora and fauna in a water and biofilm habitat, which is geared to the existing
                                                                                                                                                                          natural habitats/biotopes.


                                                                                                                                                                          Hydraulics
                                                                                                                                                                          In terms of these guidelines, the theory of water flow characteristics in pools, filters, pipelines
                                                                                                                                                                          and other technical installations.


                                                                                                                                                                          Hydraulic load
                                                                                                                                                                          Synonym: Feed rate


                                                                                                                                                                          Hydrobotanical system
                                                                                                                                                                          Technical installation for water purification. Hydrobotanical systems are designed submerged
                                                                                                                                                                          (formerly called aqua culture, refer to 2006 edition) or emersed. Water or marsh plants and
                                                                                                                                                                          zooplankton are significant elements for water purification here. Hydrobotanical systems are
                                                                                                                                                                          distinguished by a good absorption of phosphorus in the case of high specific population rates.

                                                                                                                                                                          Capillary block
                                                                                                                                                                          Design of the bank formation that prevents water loss through capillary effects.
                                                                                                                                                                           shore line


                                                                                                                                                                          Consumption
                                                                                                                                                                          Process and dimension of the food intake by organisms that feed on preformed organic sub-
                                                                                                                                                                          stances.


                                                                                                                                                                          Mechanical filtration
                                                                                                                                                                          Process for the mechanical removal of particulate components (e.g. leaves, solid matter).


                                                                                                                                                                          Natural pool
                                                                                                                                                                          Synonym: Swimming and bathing pond


                                                                                                                                                                          Swimming area
                                                                                                                                                                          The area designated for swimming. E.g. this includes areas for swimmers and non-swimmers
                                                                                                                                                                          as well as entry points.
                                                                                                                                                                          Synonyms: Swimming, bathing zone


                                                                                                                                                                          Plants
                                                                                                                                                                               Emersed plants
                                                                                                                                                                               Aquatic plants with rooted plant characteristics, helophytes

                                                                                                                                                                               Helophytes
                                                                                                                                                                               Aquatic plants that usually root under water and grow with emergent shoots.

                                                                                                                                                                               Hydrophytes
                                                                                                                                                                               Aquatic plants that usually root under water. With floating or submerged leaves.

                                                                                                                                                                               Macrophytes (aquatic)
                                                                                                                                                                               Plants in the water that can be seen by the naked eye, including vascular plants, moss,
                                                                                                                                                                               stonewort (charophyceae) and macroalgae. Macrophytes are divided into emersed and
                                                                                                                                                                               submergent plants.

                                                                                                                                                                               Regenerative plants
                                                                                                                                                                               In terms of these guidelines, plants for water purification. There is a distinction made
                                                                                                                                                                               between marsh plants (helophytes) and submergent aquatic plants (submergent hydro-
                                                                                                                                                                               phytes).

                                                                                                                                                                               Submerged plants
                                                                                                                                                                               Underwater plants with in part amphibian characteristics, hydrophytes

                                                                                                                                                                          Plankton
                                                                                                                                                                          Tiny organisms that live in the water and are carried along (drift) with the tides and currents.

                                                                                                                                                                                 Phytoplankton
                                                                                                                                                                                 Photoautotrophic organisms living in water, drifting with the flow of tides and currents
                                                                                                                                                                                 (e.g. green algae, blue-green algae, dinoflagellates).

                                                                                                                                                                                 Zooplankton
                                                                                                                                                                                 Animal organisms living in water that drift with the flow of tides and currents.


                                                                                                                                                                          Primary production
                                                                                                                                                                          The primary production takes place through plants, algae and autotrophic bacteria that cause
                                                                                                                                                                          an increase in biomass (producers). The process of photosynthesis caused by plants, algae
                                                                                                                                                                          and blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) leads to CO2, water and light breaking down biomass.
                                                                                                                                                                          Instead of light, chemolithoautotrophic microorganisms in the water use inorganic substances
                                                                                                                                                                          such as iron, manganese, sulphur or nitrogen compounds and water and CO 2 to produce bio-
                                                                                                                                                                          mass.


                                                                                                                                                                          Pore velocity
                                                                                                                                                                          Real average flow velocity that is derived from the assumption that water flows through the
                                                                                                                                                                          total pore space (total pore space = effective usable pore space).
                                                                                                                                                                          Note: the term flow velocity is used in Switzerland and Austria.



                                                                                                                                                                          Regeneration area
                                                                                                                                                                           Purification area


                                                                                                                                                                          Natural swimming pool
                                                                                                                                                                          Pool system designed especially for swimming. It is sealed against the subsoil and includes
                                                                                                                                                                          the swimming area and the regeneration area, and it has defined requirements in terms of
                                                                                                                                                                          water quality. The water is cleansed biologically, furthermore possibly physically or physi-
                                                                                                                                                                          cally/chemically as well; referred to in the following as "natural pool".
                                                                                                                                                                          Synonyms: e.g. natural pool


                                                                                                                                                                          Skimmer
                                                                                                                                                                          Technical installation to remove debris from the water surface. A distinction is made between
                                                                                                                                                                          round skimmers and wall skimmers. Skimmers tolerate a fluctuating water level (= flexible
                                                                                                                                                                          overflow weir).


                                                                                                                                                                          Sedimentation
                                                                                                                                                                          The transport of organic and inorganic particles to the bottom of the water of natural swim-
                                                                                                                                                                          ming ponds.


                                                                                                                                                                          Constructed wetlands
                                                                                                                                                                           Substrate filter

                                                                                                                                                                          Autotrophy
                                                                                                                                                                          Degree of primary plant production; also refer to Appendix 8.


                                                                                                                                                                          Waterside
                                                                                                                                                                          In terms of these guidelines the transition area between the water surface and the surrounding
                                                                                                                                                                          land. Because of the formation of the waterside, a loss of water due to capillary action as well
                                                                                                                                                                          as the induction of water by means of surface water must be prevented.

                                                                                                                                                                          Overflow weir
                                                                                                                                                                          Inflexible overflow weir, installation for water extraction from the water surface. Does not toler-

                                                                                                                                                                          Flexible overflow weir:  Skimmer (tolerates water level fluctuation)


                                                                                                                                                                          Recirculation rate
                                                                                                                                                                          The frequency with which the water in the swimming area flows through the regeneration area
                                                                                                                                                                          within a certain period of time.


                                                                                                                                                                          Legal duty to maintain safety
                                                                                                                                                                          Owner’s duty to take the required precautions, to prevent third-party risks.


                                                                                                                                                                          Water

                                                                                                                                                                              Water in the swimming area

                                                                                                                                                                                 Fill-up water
                                                                                                                                                                                 Water that is added to the system from the outside for initial filling of the system or for
                                                                                                                                                                                 topping up at a later stage (e. g. for compensation of evaporation and water loss caused
                                                                                                                                                                                 by swimmers).

                                                                                                                                                                                 Overall water
                                                                                                                                                                                 Water from all areas of the natural pool.

                                                                                                                                                                                 Surface water
                                                                                                                                                                                 Runoff water that flows off fortified and unfortified surfaces and if applicable, has an effect

                                                                                                                                                                                 Pure water
                                                                                                                                                                                 Water only immediately after the biological water purification prior to being fed into the
                                                                                                                                                                                 swimming area.

                                                                                                                                                                                 Water from the swimming area that is conveyed to the purification system.

                                                                                                                                                                          Procedure to ensure that the requirements in terms of a suitable water quality are satisfied.

                                                                                                                                                                          4        Legal and normative framework conditions

                                                                                                                                                                          The legal basis for the planning and construction of private natural swimming pools is formed
                                                                                                                                                                          by various acts and regulations, the significance of which is briefly outlined below.


                                                                                                                                                                          4.1      Acts, ordinances


                                                                                                                                                                          4.1.1    Federal level

                                                                                                                                                                          German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch - BGB)
                                                                                                                                                                          The BGB rulings relating to civil law do not contain specific definitions relating to natural swim-
                                                                                                                                                                          ming pools. However, they represent the legal basis upon which legal matters are addressed,
                                                                                                                                                                          in particular in the field of liability law (Book 2 of the BGB: law of obligations, legal duty to
                                                                                                                                                                          maintain safety – for this please refer to 8.1.4, laws pertaining to work contracts (Book 2 of the
                                                                                                                                                                          BGB) and the law concerning the respective interests of neighbours (Book 3 of the BGB: prop-
                                                                                                                                                                          erty law).


                                                                                                                                                                          German Federal Building Code (Baugesetzbuch, BauGB) with Land Use Ordinance
                                                                                                                                                                          (Baunutzungsverordnung – BauNVO)
                                                                                                                                                                          The land use planning and development plan are drawn up based on the Federal Building
                                                                                                                                                                          Code. The permissibility of a private natural pool as a structural installation (e. g. compliance
                                                                                                                                                                          with the building line or building restriction line) results from these plans in conjunction with the
                                                                                                                                                                          BauNVO and the zonal classifications contained therein. The obligation for approval results
                                                                                                                                                                          from the respective regional codes of the German federal states.


                                                                                                                                                                          Water Resources Act (Wasserhaushaltsgesetz – WHG)
                                                                                                                                                                          According to the Water Resources Act, a usage of waterbodies is subject to a permit or ap-
                                                                                                                                                                          proval, whereby the term "usage of waterbodies" includes, among other things, the extraction
                                                                                                                                                                          of water from surface waterbodies or from the groundwater, the "discharge of substances into
                                                                                                                                                                          surface waterbodies“ and the "discharge of substances into the groundwater“.


                                                                                                                                                                          Drinking Water Ordinance (Trinkwasserverordnung – TrinkwV)
                                                                                                                                                                          The Drinking Water Ordinance contains requirements relating to the quality of water for human
                                                                                                                                                                          consumption, drinking water purification as well as monitoring by the health authorities. Fur-
                                                                                                                                                                          thermore, the ordinance specifies the duties of owners and operators of water supply systems.


                                                                                                                                                                          Federal Nature Conservation Act (Bundesnaturschutzgesetz – BNatSchG)
                                                                                                                                                                          As structural installations, natural pools can represent an interference with the natural environ-
                                                                                                                                                                          ment.

                                                                                                                                                                          4.1.2      Regional level (federal states)

                                                                                                                                                                          There are acts and ordinances at regional level that can affect natural pools. Part of them
                                                                                                                                                                          substantiate the framework legislation at federal level. It is therefore important to check which
                                                                                                                                                                          laws apply in the relevant federal state for every building project. Here, we merely offer an
                                                                                                                                                                          overview of the most important acts and ordinances that can contain relevant rulings


                                                                                                                                                                          Regional building codes of the federal states (Landesbauordnungen – LBO) with exec-
                                                                                                                                                                          utive orders
                                                                                                                                                                          The regional building codes of the federal states contain rulings that govern whether or not
                                                                                                                                                                          building projects are subject to approval. As natural pools can be structural installations subject
                                                                                                                                                                          to approval (e. g. depending on size, volume and depth), the approval process is defined by
                                                                                                                                                                          the respective regional building code of the relevant federal state. These rulings can vary de-
                                                                                                                                                                          pending on the federal state.


                                                                                                                                                                          Federal states’ regional water acts
                                                                                                                                                                          The federal states’ regional water acts differentiate the rulings of the Water Resources Act and
                                                                                                                                                                          can contain rulings that affect the usage of waterbodies


                                                                                                                                                                          Laws concerning the respective interests of neighbours
                                                                                                                                                                          One of the objectives of laws concerning the respective interests of neighbours is to protect
                                                                                                                                                                          neighbours against disturbances. They may also contain rulings relevant to natural pools, e.g.
                                                                                                                                                                          regarding:
                                                                                                                                                                          •     distances between structural installations (e. g. natural pools);
                                                                                                                                                                          •     distances between plantings;
                                                                                                                                                                          •     enclosure, screening;
                                                                                                                                                                          •     protection against damaging influences as a result of rainwater or water in general (e. g.
                                                                                                                                                                                flooding).

                                                                                                                                                                          Some of the German federal states do not have any laws concerning the respective interests
                                                                                                                                                                          of neighbours. In this case, the provisions defined in the German Civil Code (Book 3 of the
                                                                                                                                                                          BGB: property law) or the definitions of the relevant regional execution laws of the federal
                                                                                                                                                                          states for the implementation of the BGB.

                                                                                                                                                                          Furthermore, local statutes must be taken into account.


                                                                                                                                                                          Nature conservation laws
                                                                                                                                                                          The nature conservation laws of the federal states contain definitions governing the interven-
                                                                                                                                                                          tion rulings of the Federal Nature Conservation Act. Each case needs to be individually clarified
                                                                                                                                                                          with the relevant authorities, in order to determine the extent to which any preservation
                                                                                                                                                                          measures (compensation, replacement) are required.

                                                                                                                                                                          5       Types of natural pools I - V

                                                                                                                                                                          Irrespective of the design and/or construction method, natural pool types are distinguished
                                                                                                                                                                          according to the type and scope of technical systems used to achieve a flow through the swim-
                                                                                                                                                                          ming area and regeneration area in accordance with Table 1.

                                                                                                                                                                          With all pool types, it can be assumed that good water quality can be achieved provided there
                                                                                                                                                                          is proper planning, construction and maintenance taking limnological principles into account.
                                                                                                                                                                          The decision as to which type is most suitable must be made taking consultation with the plan-
                                                                                                                                                                          ning expert into account and after weighing up the personal requirements of the pool owner
                                                                                                                                                                          and the conditions on location. In this phase, extensive consultation must take place; please
                                                                                                                                                                          also refer to Appendix 3.

                                                                                                                                                                          Furthermore, the following must be observed:

                                                                                                                                                                          • The intensity of use, the regeneration area and the technical installations must be coordi-
                                                                                                                                                                            nated with each other.
                                                                                                                                                                          • Water features, fountains, brooks, etc. do not count as technical installations for water pu-
                                                                                                                                                                            rification.

                                                                                                                                                                          6        Biology and chemistry of the natural pool


                                                                                                                                                                          6.1      Limnological principles

                                                                                                                                                                          Limnology is the scientific study of inland freshwater systems as eco systems.

                                                                                                                                                                          Natural pools are artificially created eco systems in which the conditions found in natural wa-
                                                                                                                                                                          terbodies are replicated, developed and optimized. The ecological conditions in natural pools
                                                                                                                                                                          are characterized by the local biocenoses and the complex feeding chains these organisms
                                                                                                                                                                          are part of. The development of the body of water is defined by the quality of the fill-up water
                                                                                                                                                                          as well as by the conditions relating to location and use (e. g. exposure, the introduction of
                                                                                                                                                                          organic and/or inorganic substances).

                                                                                                                                                                          Given that natural pools do not have a natural discharge, they can be seen as a storage for all
                                                                                                                                                                          substances brought into them. The substances brought in by users themselves while bathing
                                                                                                                                                                          or swimming must be deemed essential in this regard. To ensure a consistent good water
                                                                                                                                                                          quality, these substances and other substances brought in must be removed from the surface
                                                                                                                                                                          of the water and from the water itself and be bound and/ or removed in the regeneration areas.

                                                                                                                                                                          Specific limnological processes – i.e. ecological processes within the water – take place in all
                                                                                                                                                                          natural pools. The relevance of these processes differs for the different types of natural pools.
                                                                                                                                                                          The following must be observed:
                                                                                                                                                                          • nutrients (particularly phosphorus and nitrogen);
                                                                                                                                                                          • biocenoses of phytoplankton (floating algae) and zooplankton (small crustaceans e.g. “wa-
                                                                                                                                                                            ter fleas”) and other single-celled and multi-cellular organisms;
                                                                                                                                                                          • aquatic plants
                                                                                                                                                                          • biofilm


                                                                                                                                                                          6.2      Significance of biocenoses


                                                                                                                                                                          6.2.1    Algae and biofilm

                                                                                                                                                                          Algae are a normal part of the biocenosis of the “plant producers” in natural pools. The water
                                                                                                                                                                          influenced by light is populated by phytoplankton (floating algae), while thread-like forms
                                                                                                                                                                          (thread algae) and other substrate-binding algae (e.g. rock algae) grow on the surfaces (plastic
                                                                                                                                                                          sheets, plant substrates). At the same time, there are a multitude of transitional stages.

                                                                                                                                                                          Algae are in every natural pond depending on the type of water purification and nutrient con-
                                                                                                                                                                          tent. There is a need for action if there is a highly increased population and/or unusually high
                                                                                                                                                                          algae blooms, which e.g. have a negative effect on potential uses or the growth of desired
                                                                                                                                                                          plants. Algae development depends on the availability of nutrients (primarily phosphorus).
                                                                                                                                                                          Given that thread algae can absorb phosphates extremely effectively, it is often not possible
                                                                                                                                                                          to detect dissolved phosphorous compounds in a body of water.

                                                                                                                                                                          Biofilm develops on all damp and wet surfaces and can also be visible. It consists of algae,
                                                                                                                                                                          bacteria and other microorganisms and must also be deemed a natural component of natural
                                                                                                                                                                          pools. Biofilm is essentially for water purification in the regeneration areas. Biofilm poses a
                                                                                                                                                                          danger of slipping in the swimming area, which must be counteracted using respective
                                                                                                                                                                          measures (e.g. cleaning, surface quality). The population of biofilm is increased in areas with
                                                                                                                                                                          high inflow.

                                                                                                                                                                          6.2.2    Plants

                                                                                                                                                                          Plants are used in natural pools as elements of water purification and “decorative” elements.
                                                                                                                                                                          In the natural pool types I to III the significance of aquatic plants is greater than for the natural
                                                                                                                                                                          pool types IV and V. In addition to the direct absorption of nutrients through the roots, the plants
                                                                                                                                                                          enlarge the colonization area and improve the environmental conditions for the biofilm. At the
                                                                                                                                                                          same time, a distinction is made between submerged (aquatic plants living beneath the water
                                                                                                                                                                          surface) and emersed (marsh plants/helophytes) macrophytes.

                                                                                                                                                                          Submerged macrophytes can absorb effective nutrients from the water due to the large contact
                                                                                                                                                                          surface.

                                                                                                                                                                          Emersed macrophytes are used in substrate filters to support water purification. When planting
                                                                                                                                                                          in filter elements, please make sure that the plant’s root system does not compromise the
                                                                                                                                                                          functionality of the filter. Emersed macrophytes used in ground bodies with no flow only have
                                                                                                                                                                          a minor effect on water purification.

                                                                                                                                                                          When selecting plants, the locational conditions regarding the type, particularly the aspired low
                                                                                                                                                                          nutrient concentrations as well as plant-sociological properties must be considered. Further-
                                                                                                                                                                          more, plant species native to Central Europe must be favoured and in particular, no invasive
                                                                                                                                                                          alien species (non-indigenous species) are to be used.


                                                                                                                                                                          6.2.3    Zooplankton and macrozoobenthos

                                                                                                                                                                          In principle, natural pools are populated by zooplankton (among others rotifers, small crusta-
                                                                                                                                                                          ceans such as “water fleas”) and macrozoobenthos (among others snails, insect larvae) or-
                                                                                                                                                                          ganisms.

                                                                                                                                                                          Zooplankton populates the water body and there, effectively filters phytoplankton and bacteria
                                                                                                                                                                          from the water. The number of organisms is defined by the type of water purification and from
                                                                                                                                                                          the food supply. In the natural pool types I to III, the population density and therefore, the
                                                                                                                                                                          influence on the water purification is larger than in the natural pool types IV and V.

                                                                                                                                                                          The macrozoobenthos mostly populate surfaces (e.g. pool walls, substrate areas) in the natu-
                                                                                                                                                                          ral pool. Many species feed on biofilm, algae and plant remains, others in turn predatorily feed
                                                                                                                                                                          on these species. Ultimately, a high number of macrozoobenthos organisms indicates a high
                                                                                                                                                                          degree of plant productivity.


                                                                                                                                                                          6.2.4    Fish, amphibians, reptiles

                                                                                                                                                                          There can be no fish in the natural pool because fish that live close to the ground remobilize
                                                                                                                                                                          the sediments due to their way of diving and searching for food and in doing so, accelerate the
                                                                                                                                                                          bioconversion in the natural pool. The excrements produced by fish will increase the eutroph-
                                                                                                                                                                          ication of the natural pool. This can lead to significant algae growth.

                                                                                                                                                                          Fish that populate the water can reduce stocks of zooplankton (primarily large "water fleas" –
                                                                                                                                                                          daphnia) so severely that they are no longer capable of effectively decimating unwanted phy-
                                                                                                                                                                          toplankton and biologically cleaning the water.

                                                                                                                                                                          Amphibians (toads, frogs, salamanders) appear routinely in natural pools; reptiles rarely. The
                                                                                                                                                                          larvae of the toads and frogs (tadpoles) are useful because they effectively graze on the algae
                                                                                                                                                                          growth on surfaces. Many species are subject to strict legal protection provisions (cf. Federal
                                                                                                                                                                          Nature Conservation Act - Bundesnaturschutzgesetz - § 44 sec. 1) and can neither be intro-
                                                                                                                                                                          duced nor removed or killed.
                                                                                                                                                                          Pools and skimmers must have technical and/or constructive options of exiting.

                                                                                                                                                                          6.3      Significance of the nutrients phosphorus and nitrogen

                                                                                                                                                                          Phosphorus is an essential component of all living organisms in the water. Special attention
                                                                                                                                                                          must be paid to the nutrient phosphorus as its availability is generally at the minimum end of
                                                                                                                                                                          the scale and it is therefore the nutrient which limits plant production. Despite high and abun-
                                                                                                                                                                          dant concentrations of nitrogen, carbon and light, excessive growth of algae can be prevented
                                                                                                                                                                          if the concentration of phosphorus in the natural pool is very low. Phosphorus cannot “escape
                                                                                                                                                                          through the air” as nitrogen can.

                                                                                                                                                                          The following forms of phosphorus can be found in natural pools:
                                                                                                                                                                          • bound in organisms as well as dead organic substances and mineral components;
                                                                                                                                                                          • in dissolved organic compounds and as
                                                                                                                                                                          • inorganic dissolved orthophosphate (PO43-).

                                                                                                                                                                          The analyses of the total content of phosphorus [Ptotal] and orthophosphate are important for
                                                                                                                                                                          the evaluation of the water quality.

                                                                                                                                                                          The ratio between Ptotal and orthophosphate are changing by different aspects like seasons,
                                                                                                                                                                          light presentation, water depth etc.

                                                                                                                                                                          Nitrogen is a key plant “growth motor”, also for algae. It is an essential part of all living organ-
                                                                                                                                                                          isms (composition of proteins and nucleic acids). However, additional nitrogen compounds will
                                                                                                                                                                          enter the water during the life of the pool (e. g. urea from urine and uric acid from bird faeces).
                                                                                                                                                                          Both substances are broken down relatively quickly by bacteria, and ammonium is released in
                                                                                                                                                                          the process. Ammonium and nitrate are both partially absorbed directly by bacteria and aquatic
                                                                                                                                                                          plants (e. g. vegetation on the bank, submergent plants) and incorporated in biomass. Other
                                                                                                                                                                          nitrogen compounds are introduced through precipitation. Ammonium can also be converted
                                                                                                                                                                          into nitrite and nitrate via so-called nitrogen-fixing bacteria or it can even be removed from the
                                                                                                                                                                          system as gaseous nitrogen which is produced during bacterial denitrification. If necessary,
                                                                                                                                                                          nitrogen can also be added in a plant usable form.


                                                                                                                                                                          6.4      Importance of hygiene

                                                                                                                                                                          Common rules of hygiene also apply when using natural pools, to avoid the introduction of
                                                                                                                                                                          pathogens. In particular, the following applies to natural pools:
                                                                                                                                                                          • Swimmers should shower before entering the pool, to reduce the amount of dirt, sweat, etc.
                                                                                                                                                                            that is introduced into the pool;
                                                                                                                                                                          • The pool should not be used by persons with contagious diseases, open wounds or diar-
                                                                                                                                                                            rhoea;
                                                                                                                                                                          • Mammals (e.g. dogs) should be kept away from the water;
                                                                                                                                                                          • Waste should be kept away from the installation, to prevent the attraction of vermin (e.g.
                                                                                                                                                                            rats);
                                                                                                                                                                          • Water birds should be kept away from natural pools as the excrements of these birds can
                                                                                                                                                                            cause hygiene problems. Furthermore, their behaviour (e. g. diving and searching for food)
                                                                                                                                                                            and the substances they introduce, e. g. nutrients, fish spawn, can have a significantly det-
                                                                                                                                                                            rimental effect on the appearance of the natural pool and its ability to function as intended.

                                                                                                                                                                          • Other hygiene risks can result from the simultaneous presence of snails and water birds as
                                                                                                                                                                            they are among the hosts of certain types of cercaria (cercaria = larva stage of trematodes).
                                                                                                                                                                            Cercariae cause so-called swimming or bathing dermatitis.
                                                                                                                                                                            The following measures should be taken to prevent cercariae:
                                                                                                                                                                            - Water birds should be scared away promptly;
                                                                                                                                                                            - the snail population in the swimming area should be kept as low as possible. In the

                                                                                                                                                                          6.5     Importance of the intensity of use

                                                                                                                                                                          The type and size of the swimming area and regeneration area must be adapted to the behav-
                                                                                                                                                                          iour of the users. A minimum water volume of 10 m³ per user is recommended.

                                                                                                                                                                          7        Quality requirements


                                                                                                                                                                          7.1      Requirements pertaining to water quality


                                                                                                                                                                          7.1.1    Fill-up water

                                                                                                                                                                          The fill-up water plays a very important role, both when initially filling up the pool and when
                                                                                                                                                                          water is topped up, e. g. in order to compensate for evaporation. Tap water, well water and
                                                                                                                                                                          rainwater are all potentially suitable as fill-up water.

                                                                                                                                                                          Due to the way drinking water is currently purified, it may already contain such high phosphorus
                                                                                                                                                                          levels that it is no longer suitable as fill-up water unless further purification takes place. Fur-
                                                                                                                                                                          thermore, the phosphorus content may vary during the course of the year.

                                                                                                                                                                          Well water can contain increased nutrient contents, e.g. due to agricultural runoffs or high
                                                                                                                                                                          contents of iron or other substances.

                                                                                                                                                                          Rainwater cannot be introduced directly because of the atmospheric substance deposition,
                                                                                                                                                                          e.g. on roofs. Furthermore, water should not be used if larger amounts of metal (zinc, copper)
                                                                                                                                                                          were used because these lead to an increased concentration of heavy metals in the water. If
                                                                                                                                                                          water is taken from cisterns, floating discharge is recommended.

                                                                                                                                                                          Surface water from rivers and lakes cannot be introduced.

                                                                                                                                                                          The suitability of fill-up water must always be checked. In some cases, a more extensive water
                                                                                                                                                                          purification process is required.

                                                                                                                                                                          7.2.4    Plant substrates

                                                                                                                                                                          Plant substrates are used for submerge plants or in hydrobotanical systems. There is no flow
                                                                                                                                                                          through them.
                                                                                                                                                                          Plant substrates must be as free as possible from organic parts (risk of decay, uncontrolled
                                                                                                                                                                          nutrient release).
                                                                                                                                                                          It is best to use fine-grained material because coarser material usually makes rooting and plant
                                                                                                                                                                          development more difficult. The largest grain should not exceed 8 mm. If there is a high per-
                                                                                                                                                                          centage of silt and clay, it may be necessary to provide a cover of granular mineral materials
                                                                                                                                                                          (e.g. gravel 2/8 mm), to prevent turbidity in the event of strong water movement.
                                                                                                                                                                          A certain percentage of elutable phosphorus is preferable for species-appropriate plant devel-
                                                                                                                                                                          opment; if necessary, a barrier (cap) can be installed that limits the release of phosphorus into
                                                                                                                                                                          the water.


                                                                                                                                                                          7.2.5    Granular materials and additional components installed in the pool

                                                                                                                                                                          Granular materials > 4 mm that are used in the pool (e.g. in the area of the edge of the pool,
                                                                                                                                                                          ground coverings, decorative surfaces) should not exceed a level of elutable phosphorus of
                                                                                                                                                                          5 mg P/kg granular material; also refer to Appendix 2.

                                                                                                                                                                          When using additional components installed in the pool (e.g. steps, erratic blocks, walls, syn-
                                                                                                                                                                          thetics) as well as during their care and maintenance, please make sure that they release the
                                                                                                                                                                          smallest amount of phosphorus into the water as possible. On this note, porous surfaces
                                                                                                                                                                          should be avoided.

                                                                                                                                                                          The surrounding conditions that have an effect on construction, maintenance and usage par-

                                                                                                                                                                          •   risk of flooding;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   climatic and air hygiene conditions;

                                                                                                                                                                          •   high voltage cables, other overhead power lines;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   topography, relief;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   use of adjacent surfaces (e.g. agriculture, industry).


                                                                                                                                                                          8.1.2     Location within the property

                                                                                                                                                                          Natural pools are water systems that can be enjoyed all year round and that can be used to
                                                                                                                                                                          enhance the design of a property. The location should be chosen so that the natural pool be-
                                                                                                                                                                          comes integrated into the overall design concept of the property.

                                                                                                                                                                          At the same time, special attention must be given to:
                                                                                                                                                                          • definitions in the development plan, state building code, local specifications;
                                                                                                                                                                          • existing buildings;
                                                                                                                                                                          • supply and disposal installations and their connection;
                                                                                                                                                                          • connection to other forms of use;
                                                                                                                                                                          • tree inventory (risk of introduction of leaves, pollen, blossom, fruit, root pressure);
                                                                                                                                                                          • work required to protect against ground movement and to secure embankments, which
                                                                                                                                                                            among other things depends on the topography (steep embankments);
                                                                                                                                                                          • risk of introduction of surface water/securing of the drainage of surface water, above all
                                                                                                                                                                            taking heavy rainfall into account;
                                                                                                                                                                          • sun, shade and wind conditions. At the same time, these aspects must be considered not
                                                                                                                                                                            only in terms of their impact on the well-being of people using the pool but also in terms of
                                                                                                                                                                            biological developments and the water (e. g. cleaning, evaporation, temperature).

                                                                                                                                                                          8.1.4    Legal duty to maintain safety, enclosure

                                                                                                                                                                          There is neither a general liability corpus delicti for the legal duty to maintain safety nor a legal
                                                                                                                                                                          definition of the term "legal duty to maintain safety”. The idea of legal duty to maintain safety
                                                                                                                                                                          has been developed in case law as a sub-aspect of the general delict liability in accordance
                                                                                                                                                                          with 823 German Civil Code (BGB) or, in cases where the legal duty to maintain safety is to
                                                                                                                                                                          be asserted sovereignly, also of the official liability according to § 839 German Civil Code
                                                                                                                                                                          (BGB).

                                                                                                                                                                          Every property owner is subject to the legal duty to maintain safety and this also applies to
                                                                                                                                                                          private natural pools. This legal duty to maintain safety applies both during the construction
                                                                                                                                                                          phase and after completion of the natural pool. The owner of the property has the primary
                                                                                                                                                                          responsibility. During the construction phase, the person to whom the management of the site
                                                                                                                                                                          has been transferred, i. e. the planning expert or supplier, is also responsible in addition to the
                                                                                                                                                                          property owner.

                                                                                                                                                                          In Germany, there are no special laws, directives or top-level judicial decisions that contain
                                                                                                                                                                          concrete requirements in terms of the safety measures that should be taken for natural pools.


                                                                                                                                                                                                                           37
This release version is your personal version. The forwarding of this file, or individual excerpts, to third parties is only permitted with the express consent of FLL!
                                                                                                                                                                          The nature and scope of the safety measures are decisions which need to be taken on a case-
                                                                                                                                                                          by-case basis.

                                                                                                                                                                          According to case law, the following basic principles should be taken into account, to make a
                                                                                                                                                                          natural pool safe (the quoted verdicts relate to decorative ponds):

                                                                                                                                                                          • There is no duty to maintain legal safety in the face of persons who access the property
                                                                                                                                                                            without authorization. This does not apply to children. In order to reduce the risks for chil-
                                                                                                                                                                            dren, it must usually be ensured that children cannot gain access to the property or have
                                                                                                                                                                            an accident in the pool, e. g. by enclosing the property;
                                                                                                                                                                          • pools on private property – regardless of the type and size – represent a hazard particularly
                                                                                                                                                                            for very small children (BGH - Federal Court of Justice - verdict dated 20/9/1994);
                                                                                                                                                                          • every property owner must take effective and lasting measures to protect children against
                                                                                                                                                                            accidents, if said owner knows or should know that they use his/her property – whether
                                                                                                                                                                            being authorized or not – for playing and there is a risk that they could be harmed in the
                                                                                                                                                                            process (BGH, verdict dated 20/9/1994);
                                                                                                                                                                          • safety measures must be more and more effective the greater the appeal the pool has to
                                                                                                                                                                            children (e. g. splashing brook, stepping stones).
                                                                                                                                                                          • absolute protection against any abstract danger does not have to be provided. Only safety
                                                                                                                                                                            measures are required that would have to be deemed sufficient by an intelligent and cau-
                                                                                                                                                                            tious person, to protect other persons against harm and that could be reasonably expected
                                                                                                                                                                            of him/her under the circumstances (BGH, verdict dated 20/9/1994);
                                                                                                                                                                          • it is not automatically necessary to erect an insurmountable obstacle. A hedge or compara-
                                                                                                                                                                            ble barrier is sufficient in principle if children have not played on the property so far without
                                                                                                                                                                            being authorized and if the property is clearly and unambiguously set apart from the remain-
                                                                                                                                                                            ing terrain (OLG - Higher Regional Court - Hamm, verdict dated 23/5/2001). However, if
                                                                                                                                                                            despite all warnings and instructions neighbouring children repeatedly enter the garden,
                                                                                                                                                                            special precautions will have to be taken;
                                                                                                                                                                          • as a rule, warning signs are insufficient, given that small children cannot read them.

                                                                                                                                                                          It is recommended that the owner of a natural pools takes out liability insurance or to com-
                                                                                                                                                                          municate the natural pool to the existing insurance carrier but also to secure the property. In
                                                                                                                                                                          the process, fences should comply with the following requirements, provided the respective
                                                                                                                                                                          state building code does not stipulate anything to the contrary:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   Minimum height 1 meter;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   distance between horizontal slats ≤ 4 cm;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   distance between vertical slats ≤ 11 cm;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   ground clearance ≤ 11 cm;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   door/gate with lock.

                                                                                                                                                                          Furthermore, the design of the fence should be chosen to ensure it does not offer children any
                                                                                                                                                                          climbing opportunities that would allow them to scale the fence.

                                                                                                                                                                          In order to preserve mutual personal privacy, it may be expedient to provide privacy screens,
                                                                                                                                                                          planting hedges or the like (shielding against neighbours, roads, etc.).

                                                                                                                                                                          Moreover, enclosing the property – depending on the laws concerning the respective interests
                                                                                                                                                                          of neighbours – can also be demanded by neighbours.

                                                                                                                                                                          Furthermore, the safety-related information as defined in DIN EN 16582-1 must be observed.

                                                                                                                                                                          The safety-related information as defined in DIN EN 16582-1 must be attached to the operating
                                                                                                                                                                          and maintenance manual.

                                                                                                                                                                          9.2      Separation of swimming area and regeneration area

                                                                                                                                                                          Natural pools must be separated into a swimming area and a regeneration area.

                                                                                                                                                                          The area used by swimmers must be kept separate from the regeneration area by at least an
                                                                                                                                                                          inundated barrier. The moving/slipping of rock fill and substrates in the swimming area must
                                                                                                                                                                          also be continuously prevented using structural measures.

                                                                                                                                                                          In the natural pool types I – III barriers must be installed in a manner that minimizes turbulences
                                                                                                                                                                          and water movement in the regeneration area.

                                                                                                                                                                          The separation must be structurally durable and stable.

                                                                                                                                                                          For example the separation can be executed by means of earth modelling, wood, natural stone,
                                                                                                                                                                          concrete, dimension stone or synthetic/stainless steel elements.

                                                                                                                                                                          The natural pool types IV and V allow for different designs.


                                                                                                                                                                          9.3      Sealing

                                                                                                                                                                          Natural pools must be durably sealed against the subsoil to prevent both water from escaping
                                                                                                                                                                          from the natural pool into the subsoil and groundwater or stratum water from entering the nat-
                                                                                                                                                                          ural pool.

                                                                                                                                                                          9.6       Regeneration area

                                                                                                                                                                          The regeneration areas laid out differently according to the natural pool type. You can find

                                                                                                                                                                          A prerequisite for the ideal efficacy of regeneration areas is a fully engaged flow. That ensures

                                                                                                                                                                          10        Water purification


                                                                                                                                                                          10.1      General information

                                                                                                                                                                          Water purification is carried out by biological, physical and physical-chemical processes. The
                                                                                                                                                                          following applications are deemed unsuitable and are impermissible because they can endan-
                                                                                                                                                                          ger the operation of the purification technique:
                                                                                                                                                                          • disinfectants (e.g. chlorine, chlorine dioxide, ozone);
                                                                                                                                                                          • biocides and biocidal products (e.g. insecticides, fungicides, algicides);
                                                                                                                                                                          • products containing heavy metals;
                                                                                                                                                                          • technical techniques based on silver and copper – these metals cannot be introduced in
                                                                                                                                                                            any form;
                                                                                                                                                                          • UV disinfection;
                                                                                                                                                                          • sonification.

                                                                                                                                                                          Biotechnological techniques that use the capability of living organisms to clean the water are
                                                                                                                                                                          used for water purification. The biological biocenoses responsible for this develop inde-
                                                                                                                                                                          pendently in the water as well as in substrate and root zone. There are also additional filtering
                                                                                                                                                                          properties as well depending on the selected regeneration technique.

                                                                                                                                                                          Here, the regeneration techniques are summarized in the following techniques:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   hydrobotanical systems;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   controlled slow flow through the substrate filter;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   controlled quick flow through the substrate filter;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   controlled flow through the technical unit.

                                                                                                                                                                          Other physical-chemical techniques can only be applied in addition to and together with bio-
                                                                                                                                                                          technological techniques and should only be treated as supplementary techniques, i.e. these
                                                                                                                                                                          techniques are not taken into account for the dimensioning of the water purification system.
                                                                                                                                                                          Water purification must be able to meet the requirements as defined in section 5 without these
                                                                                                                                                                          supplementary techniques.
                                                                                                                                                                          Techniques that can be used for the biological purification of the pool water are explained
                                                                                                                                                                          below. The systems and techniques described there respectively fulfil aspects of the require-
                                                                                                                                                                          ments for the purification of water.
                                                                                                                                                                          The filtration systems must operate aerobically in order to avoid a redissolution of phosphate.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.2      Regeneration areas


                                                                                                                                                                          10.2.1    Hydrobotanical systems

                                                                                                                                                                          Hydrobotanical systems are designed using submerged plants (formerly called aquaculture)
                                                                                                                                                                          or emersed plants. Aquatic or marsh plants and zooplankton are significant elements for water
                                                                                                                                                                          purification here. Hydrobotanical systems are distinguished by a good absorption of phospho-
                                                                                                                                                                          rus in the case of high density vegetal cover.

                                                                                                                                                                          Aquatic plants that live under the water (submergent hydrophytes) are preferably used for sub-
                                                                                                                                                                          mergent hydrobotanical systems. Zooplankton eliminates phytoplankton. The nutrients that are
                                                                                                                                                                          released in the process and the nutrients additionally introduced (e. g. by the swimmers) are
                                                                                                                                                                          in part absorbed via the surface of the aquatic plants (hydrophytes). However, a significant

                                                                                                                                                                          10.3     Water circulation


                                                                                                                                                                          10.3.1   Technical flow through the swimming area

                                                                                                                                                                          Water discharge
                                                                                                                                                                          Overflow weirs are used to remove the bulk of dirt particles that accumulate on the surface
                                                                                                                                                                          (e.g. leaves, pollen), so that they do not drop to the bottom. This reduces both sedimentation
                                                                                                                                                                          and excessive development of algae due to high levels of nutrients.
                                                                                                                                                                          Water should be discharged from the surface by flowing over freely. The accumulation height
                                                                                                                                                                          must be selected in a manner that allows cleaning the surface of the water as easily as possi-
                                                                                                                                                                          ble. A distinction is made:
                                                                                                                                                                          • rigid overflow weir, e.g. gully;
                                                                                                                                                                          • flexible overflow weir, e.g. skimmer.

                                                                                                                                                                          The number, type and arrangement of the water discharge/extraction devices should be cho-
                                                                                                                                                                          sen according to the volumetric recirculation current, the local conditions and the planned type
                                                                                                                                                                          of natural pool. As a guide for the required length of overflow edge, the following value can be
                                                                                                                                                                          used for flexible and rigid overflow weirs depending on the shape and type of the natural pool:
                                                                                                                                                                          1% of the surface area of the swimming area in metres.
                                                                                                                                                                          Example: Swimming area 30 m², 1% of 30 = 0.3 i. e. length of the overflow edge 0.3 m.

                                                                                                                                                                          By positioning the water discharge/extraction devices in the "local" primary wind direction and
                                                                                                                                                                          by providing carefully controlled water recirculation, a continuous flow can be achieved that
                                                                                                                                                                          assists the effective removal of dirt that has entered the system. Unhindered inflow to the dis-
                                                                                                                                                                          charge devices must be ensured. There must be direct access from the edge for cleaning
                                                                                                                                                                          purposes.

                                                                                                                                                                          There should be a device behind the water discharge/extraction unit, to hold back amphibians
                                                                                                                                                                          and leaves as well as coarse dirt. Technical and/or construction measures should be provided,
                                                                                                                                                                          to enable amphibians to exit. Overflow weirs are to be installed in a manner that the overflow
                                                                                                                                                                          edge does not deviate horizontally by more than +/- 2 mm or for overflow edges up to 1 m, by
                                                                                                                                                                          more than +/- 1 mm.


                                                                                                                                                                          Water reservoir/splash water tank
                                                                                                                                                                          Depending on the hydraulic layout of the natural pool, it may be necessary to incorporate a
                                                                                                                                                                          water reservoir, e.g as a compensating tank or splash water tank. Please refer to DGfdB R
                                                                                                                                                                          65.06 and DIN 19643-1 for dimensioning and technical equipment.

                                                                                                                                                                          A water reservoir is mandatory when using rigid overflow weirs. The water reservoir serves:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   maintaining the water level;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   to keep up the flow over rigid overflow weirs;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   as a water depot used to compensate for evaporation losses;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   absorbing the displaced water (splash water).


                                                                                                                                                                          Approximate value for the dimensioning of the water reservoir
                                                                                                                                                                          The usable volume of the splash water tank must be dimensioned so that at least 150 l per
                                                                                                                                                                          square metre can be provided to the inundated water surface.




                                                                                                                                                                                                                        54
This release version is your personal version. The forwarding of this file, or individual excerpts, to third parties is only permitted with the express consent of FLL!
                                                                                                                                                                          If an automatic feed system is installed according to DIN EN 1717 and DIN 1988 Part 100 for
                                                                                                                                                                          water replenishment, an overflow height of merely 1 cm plus water displaced by the swimmers
                                                                                                                                                                          needs to be provided.

                                                                                                                                                                          If an automatic feed system is used for the water supply, it must be set up so that it is only fed
                                                                                                                                                                          to the extent that the water level is in the bottom third of the splash water reservoir.

                                                                                                                                                                          In the case of multi-area systems, only the water surface of the area to which the overflow weir
                                                                                                                                                                          is connected is taken into account.

                                                                                                                                                                          Essential water reservoir requirements
                                                                                                                                                                          • project-related sufficient dimensioning;
                                                                                                                                                                          • accessibility and possibility of being cleaned and maintained;
                                                                                                                                                                          • an emergency overflow must be provided;
                                                                                                                                                                          • an introduction of nutrients into the water reservoir must be prevented because these nutri-
                                                                                                                                                                            ents would be available in the overall system due to the water recirculation;
                                                                                                                                                                          • an exit for animals, e.g. amphibians, must be ensured.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.3.2   Overflow

                                                                                                                                                                          An overflow must be provided at a suitable point, to discharge excess water. It must be ensured
                                                                                                                                                                          that accumulating water is discharged reliably and in a controlled manner. If it is fed into a
                                                                                                                                                                          drainage system, the drain pipe of all connected roof and open areas must be considered, to
                                                                                                                                                                          ensure a discharge without any backwater.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.3.3   Intake, discharge and feed systems

                                                                                                                                                                          Please observe DGfdB R 60.03 if an intake, discharge and/or feed system is to be installed in
                                                                                                                                                                          the swimming area.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.3.4   Water feed/water return

                                                                                                                                                                          To prevent excessive biofilm and algae growth, please make sure that there is no direct flow
                                                                                                                                                                          against bottom, wall or installation surfaces in regard to water return.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.4     Plants, planting

                                                                                                                                                                          General information

                                                                                                                                                                          Plants perform different tasks in the various types of natural pools and must be selected ac-
                                                                                                                                                                          cordingly.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.4.1   Plants in natural pool types I - III

                                                                                                                                                                          Plants in the natural pool types I - III (swimming and underwater plants, marsh and aquatic
                                                                                                                                                                          plants) contribute to the cleansing of the water and help to provide a natural ambience. Given
                                                                                                                                                                          that hardly any nutrients will be available to the plants when they are first planted, particularly
                                                                                                                                                                          strict requirements are placed on the correct choice of plants, the quality of the seed stock and
                                                                                                                                                                          the soil/substrate. Furthermore, it is extremely important that the planting itself is carried out
                                                                                                                                                                          very carefully.



                                                                                                                                                                                                                          55
This release version is your personal version. The forwarding of this file, or individual excerpts, to third parties is only permitted with the express consent of FLL!
                                                                                                                                                                          The role of the plants basically includes:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   nutrient removal/nutrient specification;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   oxygen production;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   surface for biofilm growth;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   semi-natural appearance;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   providing shade;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   habitate for zooplankton;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   substrate stabilization.

                                                                                                                                                                          As a basic principle, the relevant rules defined in DIN 18916 apply.


                                                                                                                                                                          Plant quality
                                                                                                                                                                          In accordance with DIN 18916, the plants and their packaging must meet the requirements of
                                                                                                                                                                          the "Gütebestimmungen für Stauden" (quality requirements for shrubs). Furthermore, the fol-
                                                                                                                                                                          lowing must be observed:
                                                                                                                                                                          • plants that were cultivated in a purely mineral substrate with root balls should preferably be
                                                                                                                                                                            used;
                                                                                                                                                                          • the growing substrate should be free of feeding fertiliser;
                                                                                                                                                                          • plants, substrate and growing containers must be free of foreign growth that could endanger
                                                                                                                                                                            the development of the intended vegetation and cause unwanted developments in the nat-
                                                                                                                                                                            ural pool.


                                                                                                                                                                          Vegetation support layer
                                                                                                                                                                          Refer to section 10.2.1.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.4.2    Plants in natural pool type IV

                                                                                                                                                                          If applicable, the plants in natural pool type IV only play a subordinate role in water purification.
                                                                                                                                                                          The diversity of species is severely limited here because of the locational conditions.

                                                                                                                                                                          Basically, the role of the plants here is a semi-natural appearance.


                                                                                                                                                                          Plant quality
                                                                                                                                                                          In accordance with DIN 18916, the plants and their packaging must meet the requirements of
                                                                                                                                                                          the "Gütebestimmungen für Stauden" (quality requirements for shrubs). Furthermore, the fol-
                                                                                                                                                                          lowing must be observed:
                                                                                                                                                                          • plants cultivated in a purely mineral substrate must be used; the growing substrate should

                                                                                                                                                                          10.4.3      Planting information

                                                                                                                                                                          Planting time
                                                                                                                                                                          Planting should be done during the vegetation period. The best time for planting is from the
                                                                                                                                                                          end of April/beginning of May to the middle of July.

                                                                                                                                                                          Plants with root balls should not be planted any later than the end of September.

                                                                                                                                                                          Plants that live under the water (hydrophytes, submerged plants) can be planted as soon as
                                                                                                                                                                          they are available in the spring; after that until September.


                                                                                                                                                                          Protection measures between delivery and flooding
                                                                                                                                                                          Until the plants have been planted or flooded, they require special protection against dehydra-
                                                                                                                                                                          tion and the effects of the sun and wind. Plants that have been cultivated submerged must be
                                                                                                                                                                          treated with particular care.


                                                                                                                                                                          Diversity of species, plant density
                                                                                                                                                                          The plants in natural pools are is subject to natural succession; some species can disappear,
                                                                                                                                                                          while others prevail and propagate.

                                                                                                                                                                          For plant density, the following number of plants applies as an approximate value per m² of
                                                                                                                                                                          planting surface area:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   submerged plants                                                   6 – 10 plants;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   half-height to tall marsh and aquatic plants                       3 – 5 plants;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   medium height to tall marsh and aquatic plants                     5 – 7 plants;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   lilies and lily pads                                               depending on type.

                                                                                                                                                                          The aforementioned number of plants applies to pot plants P 0.5 (500 cm³).


                                                                                                                                                                          Cutting back plants
                                                                                                                                                                          Cutting back plants belongs to routine maintenance work in a natural pool. The scope and
                                                                                                                                                                          significance depend on the type of natural pool. Cutting back plants:
                                                                                                                                                                          • prevents seeding;
                                                                                                                                                                          • keeps heavy plant growth in check;
                                                                                                                                                                          • prevents sludge from forming due to decaying leaves.

                                                                                                                                                                          Any solid stems protruding from the water on plants should not be cut until right before the
                                                                                                                                                                          spring because a desirable gas exchange can take place through them during the winter.


                                                                                                                                                                          10.4.4   Completion, status ready for acceptance inspection

                                                                                                                                                                          ing for plants until they become mature – please also refer to ATV DIN 18320 and DIN 18916.

                                                                                                                                                                          to waive this right to pre-maturity care by the supplier, the supplier must inform the purchaser
                                                                                                                                                                          about the type, scope and timing of work required. Please refer to section 11 for information
                                                                                                                                                                          about the consequences in terms of the acceptance inspection.

                                                                                                                                                                          12        Maintenance


                                                                                                                                                                          12.1      General information

                                                                                                                                                                          A natural pool will only work in the long run if it is maintained regularly and properly. This
                                                                                                                                                                          includes:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   checks (inspections);
                                                                                                                                                                          •   maintenance of the vegetation, water, substrate and visible sealing areas;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   maintenance of the structural and technical installations;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   repairs.

                                                                                                                                                                          If the owner of the pool is unwilling or incapable of performing this work him- or herself to the
                                                                                                                                                                          required extent and with the necessary professional expertise, he or she should contract a
                                                                                                                                                                          suitably qualified and reliable company to do all or part of this work for the owner.

                                                                                                                                                                          Written operating instructions must be created for the proper planning and construction of a
                                                                                                                                                                          natural pool. This includes:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   required checks;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   required maintenance, care and intervals;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   instructions for use;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   instructions for technical products in/around the natural pool.

                                                                                                                                                                          Furthermore, the purchaser must be given full verbal instructions.


                                                                                                                                                                          12.2      Checks (inspections)

                                                                                                                                                                          The following checks should be performed on a regular basis. A specialist should be consulted
                                                                                                                                                                          if necessary.
                                                                                                                                                                          • water transparency;
                                                                                                                                                                          • measurement and documentation of water chemistry;
                                                                                                                                                                          • condition of the pool edge (stability, capillary block, drainage of surface water);
                                                                                                                                                                          • unnaturally high water losses, e. g. due to an inadequate capillary block, leaks;
                                                                                                                                                                          • condition of the sealing system (presence of any stresses, protective layer, exposed areas);
                                                                                                                                                                          • proper working condition of feed and discharge pipes and overflows, technical and structural
                                                                                                                                                                            installations;
                                                                                                                                                                          • requirements resulting from maintenance work and/or care – please refer to section 12.3 –
                                                                                                                                                                            or from repair work – please refer to section 12.4;
                                                                                                                                                                          • development/growth of algae;
                                                                                                                                                                          • unusual growth of the planned vegetation (e. g. displacement of individual species due to
                                                                                                                                                                            excessive spread of other species, deficiency symptoms caused by lack of nutrients);
                                                                                                                                                                          • presence of animals (snails, fish, others);
                                                                                                                                                                          • infestation with pests.

                                                                                                                                                                          Irrespective thereof, the supplier should be instructed to perform the checks once annually for
                                                                                                                                                                          the duration of the time period in which warranty claims can be made. After that period, routine
                                                                                                                                                                          checks should be made (e.g. once a year) by a specialist.

                                                                                                                                                                          12.3    Care and maintenance

                                                                                                                                                                          Care and maintenance work must be conducted on a regular basis. They serve maintaining
                                                                                                                                                                          the pool and the reduction of nutrients and in doing so, prevent succession from progressing.
                                                                                                                                                                          Besides the work listed, additional measures can become necessary depending on the design
                                                                                                                                                                          and manufacturer’s specifications.

                                                                                                                                                                          Water care products that fall under the biocidal products regulation must be permitted for use
                                                                                                                                                                          in natural pools and can only be used temporarily. The desired biocenosis cannot be sustain-
                                                                                                                                                                          ably damaged by using the aforementioned products or other technical methods.

                                                                                                                                                                          DIN 18919 applies to other vegetation areas (outside the regeneration area).

                                                                                                                                                                          Compensation of water loss
                                                                                                                                                                          The water level must not drop below the minimum water level required for the particular type
                                                                                                                                                                          of natural pool, as damage is otherwise likely to result (e. g. poor flow through the regeneration
                                                                                                                                                                          area, pump damage).

                                                                                                                                                                          Even in a correctly installed pool, there will be certain water losses caused for example by
                                                                                                                                                                          normal use and splashing but particularly, also due to evaporation:
                                                                                                                                                                          • direct evaporation (due to the sun, wind, low relative humidity, etc., even under frosty/icy
                                                                                                                                                                            conditions) – this is significantly increased by movement of the water (e. g. due to brooks,
                                                                                                                                                                            fountains);
                                                                                                                                                                          • indirect evaporation via plants;
                                                                                                                                                                          • via stones and stone mounds that protrude from the water.

                                                                                                                                                                          On average, the water loss due to evaporation is around 2 mm per day. Under certain condi-
                                                                                                                                                                          tions it can also be as high as 12 mm per day – this corresponds to 12 l/m² of water surface
                                                                                                                                                                          area. Furthermore, this loss can increase to more than 20 mm through use of the pool and
                                                                                                                                                                          splashing (in particular, free-falling water). Please also refer to Appendix 6 for information on
                                                                                                                                                                          evaporation. If these losses are not balanced by natural precipitation, the pool system will need
                                                                                                                                                                          to be refilled – information regarding fill-up water can be found in section 7.1.1.

                                                                                                                                                                          Section 12.4 contains information about replacing large amounts or even all of the water.

                                                                                                                                                                          Appendix 2 (normative): Definition of the elutable phosphorus level in construc-
                                                                                                                                                                          tion materials


                                                                                                                                                                          Preliminary note
                                                                                                                                                                          The described method of analysis serves determining the phosphorus content in construction
                                                                                                                                                                          materials (e.g. substrates, gravels) taking bioavailability (plants, algae) into particular account.
                                                                                                                                                                          The bound water- and acid-soluble phosphorus level is defined.

                                                                                                                                                                          Principles of the process
                                                                                                                                                                          Under defined conditions the material to be analyzed is eluted as an eluent by shaking with 1
                                                                                                                                                                          M HCl. The solution-solid ratio is 2:1, the test duration 24 hours. As a result we receive a
                                                                                                                                                                          solution that can be analyzed for phosphorus according to an existing standard process. The
                                                                                                                                                                          process is based on DIN 19529:2009-01.

                                                                                                                                                                          The tests must be conducted at lab temperature (20°C ± 2°C).

                                                                                                                                                                          All results must be based on dry substance (DIN EN 13040:2008-01).

                                                                                                                                                                          Reagents/equipment
                                                                                                                                                                          • Eluent: 1.0 M HCl solution;
                                                                                                                                                                          • sieve with standard set of sieves up to 32 mm;
                                                                                                                                                                          • analytical balance, error margin 0.1 g at the most;
                                                                                                                                                                          • wide mouth jar made of PE or PP with different nominal volumes;
                                                                                                                                                                          • shaker, e.g. overhead shaker, rotational speed 5-10 min-1;
                                                                                                                                                                          • centrifuge;
                                                                                                                                                                          • filtration device with membrane filter (pore size 0.45 µm);
                                                                                                                                                                          • drying oven.

                                                                                                                                                                          Dimensioning of the experimental approach depends on grain size
                                                                                                                                                                          The process applies to materials with a grain size of 32 mm at the most.
                                                                                                                                                                          In addition to DIN 19529:2009-01, the required sample quantity is indicated for samples with a
                                                                                                                                                                          maximum grain of 16 mm.
                                                                                                                                                                          The process described in DIN 19529:2009-01 applies for material samples with a maximum
                                                                                                                                                                          grain size of > 32 mm:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   separation of the grain size > 32 mm;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   refraction;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   separation of the grain size 16/32 mm using the sieve;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   addition to the starting material in the quantity share in which the share > 32 mm was in the
                                                                                                                                                                              starting material.

                                                                                                                                                                          Sampling
                                                                                                                                                                          Sampling should be conducted according to DIN EN 932-1:1996-11.
                                                                                                                                                                          The minimum quantity is to be taken depending on the grain size (Table 14, column 2) (larger
                                                                                                                                                                          than twice the oven-dry mass for elution for retained sample).
                                                                                                                                                                          The relevant standards and good laboratory practice must be observed during the selection of
                                                                                                                                                                          the sampling device, conservation, transport and storage of the samples as well as when ob-
                                                                                                                                                                          taining the analysis samples (sample preparation).

                                                                                                                                                                          Sample preparation
                                                                                                                                                                          The samples are dried for the analysis at 50°C until constant weight and then stored airtight in
                                                                                                                                                                          plastic containers, provided they are not directly further processed.

                                                                                                                                                                          Eluate production
                                                                                                                                                                          Eluate production takes place according to DIN 19529:2009-01. The solid-solution ratio for this
                                                                                                                                                                          method is 1:2, i.e. per kg sample mass 2 litres of solution.
                                                                                                                                                                          The sample quantity to be used to produce the eluate must be weighed depending on the
                                                                                                                                                                          maximum grain size (Table 14,column 3). Plastic wide mouth jars must be used for the shake
                                                                                                                                                                          tests, whose nominal volume must be selected depending on the sample quantity (Table 14,
                                                                                                                                                                          column 4).
                                                                                                                                                                          A corresponding volume of eluent (1.0 m HCl) must be added, so that a solution-solid ratio of
                                                                                                                                                                          2:1 yields ± 0.05 l/kg. Then, the jar must be closed and a thorough mixing of the solid matter
                                                                                                                                                                          and solvent must be ensured. The sample must be shook for a duration of 24 hours in an
                                                                                                                                                                          overhead shaker at a rotational speed of 5-10 per minute.
                                                                                                                                                                          The elution of multiple partial samples is permitted while maintaining the solution-solid ratio.
                                                                                                                                                                          At the same time, the partial eluates must be immediately combined after elution.
                                                                                                                                                                          The pH value is not checked in this experimental approach.
                                                                                                                                                                          Some building materials, for instance calcareous rocks, can release gases (CO 2) during this
                                                                                                                                                                          process. In the event of severe gas formation, wait for the reaction to subside directly after
                                                                                                                                                                          adding the eluent before closing the jar; if necessary, add the eluent little by little. Carefully
                                                                                                                                                                          opening the jar briefly during the shaking process can prevent excessive pressurization. Doc-
                                                                                                                                                                          ument gas formation and opening.

                                                                                                                                                                          Liquid/solid separation
                                                                                                                                                                          The containers are removed from the shaker and placed down upright, so that suspended solid
                                                                                                                                                                          matter can settle for approximately 15 min.

                                                                                                                                                                          decanting and centrifuged (30 min. at least 2.000 g, preferably cooled. A clear eluate solution
                                                                                                                                                                          that is decanted is normally obtained for substrates with purely mineral source materials.
                                                                                                                                                                          If the supernatant solution is cloudy, it must be filtered through a 0.45 membrane filter using a
                                                                                                                                                                          vacuum or pressure filtration device.

                                                                                                                                                                          The phosphorus level in the eluate solution is determined according to DIN EN ISO 17294-
                                                                                                                                                                          2:2005-02 or DIN EN ISO 11885:2009-09.

                                                                                                                                                                          Appendix 6 (informative): Scientific information on evaporation


                                                                                                                                                                          Source: DVWK fact sheet 238/1996; Ermittlung der Verdunstung von Land- und Was-
                                                                                                                                                                          serflächen (determination of the evaporation from land and water surfaces)

                                                                                                                                                                          The conversion of water – at temperatures below the boiling point – from its liquid state or solid
                                                                                                                                                                          state (ice or snow) into its gaseous state (water vapour) is referred to as evaporation. In the
                                                                                                                                                                          process, the water vapour is absorbed by ambient air that is not yet saturated. The term evap-
                                                                                                                                                                          oration rate is used to describe the quantity of water that evaporates in a given unit of time.


                                                                                                                                                                          Factors that can influence evaporation
                                                                                                                                                                          Factors that can influence evaporation (amounts) include:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   wind speed;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   humidity;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   water temperature;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   water movement.

                                                                                                                                                                          The water temperature in turn is influenced by the following factors:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   air temperature;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   depth of the water surface;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   hydraulics (flow conditions);
                                                                                                                                                                          •   shape and expanse of the water surface;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   level of dissolved substances in the water.

                                                                                                                                                                          Methods for measuring the evaporation from water surfaces
                                                                                                                                                                          Various methods are used to measure evaporation. They provide different results that occa-
                                                                                                                                                                          sionally do not match the real quantities evaporated from water surfaces.

                                                                                                                                                                          Taking the criteria into account that determine the choice of the evaporation method, the use
                                                                                                                                                                          of evaporation containers (as a direct measuring technique) is best suited for measuring evap-
                                                                                                                                                                          oration from natural pools. They are open containers that are intended for practical use and
                                                                                                                                                                          they provide good measuring results. They are also known as evaporation pans or evaporation
                                                                                                                                                                          tanks.

                                                                                                                                                                          The following can be measured using evaporation containers:
                                                                                                                                                                          • changes in water level;
                                                                                                                                                                          • required fill-up quantities;
                                                                                                                                                                          • weight loss of the water.

                                                                                                                                                                          In practice, evaporation containers are used in different ways:
                                                                                                                                                                          •   on the surface of the terrain close to the water surfaces that are to be measured
                                                                                                                                                                          •   embedded in the ground near the water surfaces that are to be measured
                                                                                                                                                                          •   mounted on a measuring float – determined for the water surfaces to be measured;
                                                                                                                                                                          •   standing beneath a protective roof.

                                                                                                                                                                          For the measurement of evaporation from natural pools, the best method is to use an evapo-
                                                                                                                                                                          ration container mounted on a measuring float because in doing so, the evaporation container
                                                                                                                                                                          lies on the water surface of the pool and therefore, experiences (nearly) the same boundary
                                                                                                                                                                          conditions for evaporation as the actual water in the pool.

                                                                                                                                                                          Calculation of the evaporation from water surfaces
                                                                                                                                                                          The following options exist for the calculation of evaporation from water surfaces:
                                                                                                                                                                          • aerodynamic method or Dalton method;
                                                                                                                                                                          • energy balance process
                                                                                                                                                                          • combined process.

                                                                                                                                                                          According to the aerodynamic method, the calculation of evaporation can be performed as
                                                                                                                                                                          follows:

                                                                                                                                                                          Ew = f(v) · [eS(Two)-e]

                                                                                                                                                                          Ew      = evaporation from the water surface in mm/day
                                                                                                                                                                          f(v)    = function of the wind speed v
                                                                                                                                                                          eS(Two) = saturation pressure at the temperature of the water surface Two in h Pa
                                                                                                                                                                          e       = vapour pressure of the air in h Pa

                                                                                                                                                                          Appendix 7 (informative): Scientific information on cercariae


                                                                                                                                                                          What is cercarial dermatitis (swimmer’s itch)?
                                                                                                                                                                          Cercarial dermatitis is also know as “swimmer’s itch”. It can occur after swimming or wading in
                                                                                                                                                                          a body of water and causes reddened and itching wheals on the skin that can resemble mos-
                                                                                                                                                                          quito bites.

                                                                                                                                                                          The pathogen that causes cercarial dermatitis is small fork-tailed larvae of trematode worms
                                                                                                                                                                          of the genus Trichobilharzia, which normally affect water birds and cause avian schistosomia-
                                                                                                                                                                          sis. The adult trematodes release eggs into the water through duck excrements, from which a
                                                                                                                                                                          larval form results. These search for great pond snails as an intermediate host.

                                                                                                                                                                          Within 2 – 3 months the 2nd larval form develops; the fork-tailed cercaria. At water tempera-
                                                                                                                                                                          tures of around 20°C, these then leave their intermediate host during the summer (swarming
                                                                                                                                                                          time) and look for an end host. In the water, the cercariae can only survive for two or three
                                                                                                                                                                          days and while searching for a host they primarily dwell at the water surface and near plants.

                                                                                                                                                                          Humans who are also infected by swimming in freshwater are accidental hosts who are mis-
                                                                                                                                                                          takenly sought out by cercariae. The cercariae penetrate the skin but are already killed off by
                                                                                                                                                                          the immune defense system in the hypodermis. Although an affection with Trichobilharzia cer-
                                                                                                                                                                          cariae is unpleasant on account of the itching, it is not dangerous. As the swarming time and
                                                                                                                                                                          survival span of the larvae in water are very short due to their complex development cycle, the
                                                                                                                                                                          detrimental impact on enjoying the natural pool is short-lived.


                                                                                                                                                                          Scaring away water bird
                                                                                                                                                                          In order to keep the number of water birds and at the same time, the number of end hosts as
                                                                                                                                                                          low as possible around the natural pool and reduce contamination of the bathing water with
                                                                                                                                                                          excrements, water birds should be scared away promptly.

                                                                                                                                                                          Freshwater snails are an essential element in the ecosystem and should only be mechanically
                                                                                                                                                                          removed if there is a strong suspicion of cercarial infestation.

Appendix 8 (informative): Autotrophy


Autotrophy is the degree of primary plant production. In particular this includes the develop-
ment of algae, mosses and higher plants.

The primary production in the natural pool is determined by the availability of light and nutrients
as well as the temperature. The nutrient, which - as regards the necessity of primary producers
- is available in the smallest quantity (in the natural pool normally phosphorus), limits the growth
of algae and higher plants.

trophic system for natural pools. The levels indicated in the table and the associated values

nung, Bau, Instandhaltung und Betrieb von Freibädern mit biologischer Wasseraufbereitung
(2011)“ (recommendation for the planning, construction, maintenance and operation of open
air pools with biological water purification). The indicated values and descriptions can be re-

Depending on the design, concept and seasonal situation of a pool system, the total content
of phosphorus can be higher (or lower), which does not automatically indicate an immediate
problem. In such cases, please look at and assess the overall concept of the system.

Normally, natural pool types I - III are in the nutrient-poor range between oligotrophic and mes-
otrophic. Strong producing bodies of water frequently move in the slightly eutrophic range.
Here, biomass is permanently extracted in line with care work (cutting back plants, removing
algae and sediment) and consequently, nutrients are exported from the system.

An approximate value of < 0.01 mg / l Ptotal is recommended for natural pool types IV - V, which
corresponds to oligotrophic conditions according to the table presentation. This value can in-
crease for a short time however, should quickly fall back below the approximate value.

The factors that influence the trophic systems and therefore, biomass production of a natural
pool are basically:
• nutrient content of the water;
• nutrient content of the substrate and its covering (sand, gravel, biomass covering or mineral
  biomass);
• filter for natural pool type IV – V;
• external introductions/swimmers
• Condition/physical appearance of the pool.

These factors have an influence on the trophic system (care work, nutrient precipitation, biofilm
harvesting, sediment removal, cutting back plants, etc.)
The discrepancies between the desired low nutrients to prevent algae development and the
accompanying, often diminutive plant development are particularly shown in the natural pool
types IV and V. The nutrients are already filtered out of the fill-up water and reduced in the
water system by an efficient filter technology. The resulting nutrient deficiency (oligotrophic
bodies of water) leads to deficiency symptoms in the plants.
However, the still available nutrients in combination with sunlight often creates a more or less
strong algae development. An optimized provision of nutrients for the plants using a suitable
selection of substrates and plants as well as the addition of lacking nutrients (mainly nitrogen)
can improve this situation.
