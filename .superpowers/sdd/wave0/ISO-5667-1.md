### Unsupported condition grammar (non-parsing) — ~21 CRs
evaluate.ts gate grammar does NOT accept `eq`, `==`, `IS NOT NULL`, or chained
`AND`. Affected: CR-001,010 (`eq`); CR-003,004,005,007 (`==`); CR-002,008,009,011,
013,014,015,018,019,021,022,026 (`IS NOT NULL`); CR-016 (`IS NOT NULL` + chained
`AND` → **greedy-AND** class). Only CR-012/017/023/024 use `>=`/`>` numeric
comparison (supported grammar) — but 012/023/024 are cross-worksheet phantom, so
only **CR-017 (≥50 mm)** is both grammar-valid AND on-worksheet → the single
plausibly-enforceable block gate in the standard.

### #22 hand-enterable-derived / dead-table
- **K** is encoded as a free `number` engineer_input on ISO-5667-1-07 with no table
  object and no edge to the `confidence_level` enum, though the standard makes K a
  `standard_fixed` lookup keyed by confidence level (see tab-k-factor). Engineer can
  type an arbitrary K. Fix = wire K = lookup(confidence_level) from the §16.4 table.

### Inequality/verdict-as-producer / F-4 (var-vs-var)
- **Gl.2 vs Gl.3 inverse pair**: both `n` and `L` are simultaneously `is_required`
  engineer inputs AND equation outputs. The standard presents them as inverse forms
  of one relation (pick L→derive n, or pick n→derive L); the design target should be
  `derived` and the other the input. Not machine-enforced (F-4-adjacent).
- CR-023 (`n>0`) / CR-024 (`L>0`) are inequality gates on derived outputs that are
  always positive → tautological even if wired.
